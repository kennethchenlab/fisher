Our nation’s founders famously wrote that the “Laws of Nature and Nature’s God” dictate that ["all men are created equal"](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/declaration-transcript). In other words, the natural order is one of justice and equality and dignity. And so, the racial inequities we see today are not the natural order, but the result of human actions to thwart the natural state of affairs. Our society today — all the good parts and all the bad parts — is the product of millions and millions of individual decisions.

And so I mourn. For the deaths of George Floyd, Tamir Rice, Sandra Bland, and so many others. For the legacy of slavery and structural race-based discrimination that continues to inhibit [economic](https://www.pnas.org/content/early/2017/09/11/1706255114) and [academic progress](https://advances.sciencemag.org/content/5/10/eaaw7238) for African-Americans. For the hundreds of thousands to suffer the unequal burden of the current pandemic. For the social injustices that have produced the suffering and prejudice that black men and women endure at the hands of the criminal justice system. 

I can’t know what it’s like to grow up black, or Latino, or Native American, or even white. I don’t know what it was like to grow up in the inner-city, or in a Jim Crow society. I’ve never been treated like a rarity as a [tech blogger](https://youtu.be/o-_WXXVye3Y) or [astrophysicist](https://www.haydenplanetarium.org/tyson/commentary/2020-06-03-reflections-on-color-of-my-skin.php). 

But I can recognize that, as an Asian, I’m in a unique position of being a minority (and thus, at times, on the receiving end of racism), but one that can be “over-represented” (at times, on the receiving end of privilege). I can recognize that, as a scientist, I'm interested in the laws of nature, but race is a social construct has [no basis in biology](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/magazine/2018/04/race-genetics-science-africa/), and yet, as a medical professional, race can be a useful proxy for some [health risks](https://blogs.scientificamerican.com/voices/what-role-should-race-play-in-medicine/), especially in the [current pandemic](https://www.cdc.gov/coronavirus/2019-ncov/need-extra-precautions/racial-ethnic-minorities.html). 

The writers of the Declaration of Independence were not perfect —  many held slaves — but they would go on to establish a system by which our nation can continue to reform itself towards a [more perfect union](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution-transcript). Through this system of constitutional amendments, slavery was abolished; equal protection under the law was guaranteed; and universal suffrage was enacted. I'm not perfect either, but by the grace of God, I hope to grow as well. I hope to humbly accept criticism, to learn about others’ experiences, to stand up against micro- and macro-aggressions, and to advocate for equitable representation. 

Our society today is the result of individual actions in the past, but tomorrow’s society is a result of the decisions we make today. What was built by millions of human decisions can be unbuilt by millions of [new](https://bit.ly/3gC1pJA) human decisions. We can’t change what’s already happened, but we can decide what the future looks like. We can choose to do nothing and propagate the current system, or we can choose to unleash the Laws of Nature, in the words of [the prophet Amos](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/002096430005700208) and [Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr.](https://www.archives.gov/files/press/exhibits/dream-speech.pdf), “let justice roll down like waters and righteousness like a mighty stream”.
